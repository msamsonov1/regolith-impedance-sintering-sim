Executive Driving Mechanism

The core innovation is a closed-loop multi-physics microwave cavity auditor that continuously tracks the non-linear high-temperature complex dielectric impedance trajectory $Z(E)$ against absorbed cumulative energy $E$ during the $2.45\,\text{GHz}$ sintering of lunar regolith containing nanophase metallic iron ($np\text{-}\text{Fe}^0$). By identifying plateaus in impedance shifts ("Z-Stalls") caused by trapped degassing pockets and thermal hotspots, the system dynamically modulates magnetron duty cycles and phase shifters to collapse internal voids and prevent thermal runaway cracking—guaranteeing structural compressive strength exceeding $\sigma_c > 30\,\text{MPa}$ for Artemis surface construction.

1. Identification and Significance of the Innovation

Sustainable permanence on the lunar surface under the Artemis campaign requires the autonomous construction of blast-resistant landing pads, radiation and micrometeorite shielding berms, and structural pressurized habitat envelopes. Launching prefabricated construction materials from Earth carries exorbitant logistical costs (historically $>\$50,000/\text{kg}$ to lunar transit). In-Situ Resource Utilization (ISRU) via raw regolith thermal consolidation is imperative.

However, conventional radiative and conductive surface heating suffers from the extremely low thermal conductivity of unprocessed lunar vacuum regolith ($k \approx 0.002 - 0.01\,\text{W/m}\cdot\text{K}$). Surface heating causes surface crusting and boil-off before the brick interior can consolidate. Microwave sintering at $2.45\,\text{GHz}$ bypasses this through volumetric heating: lunar regolith contains sub-micron nanophase metallic iron ($np\text{-}\text{Fe}^0$) dispersed within impact agglutinitic silicate glass, which exhibits massive dipolar loss and ferromagnetic resonance.

As microwave energy is absorbed, the material's complex dielectric permittivity $\epsilon^* = \epsilon' - j\epsilon''$ changes rapidly. The power dissipation per unit volume is governed by:

$$P_{\text{diss}} = 2\pi f \epsilon_0 \epsilon'' |E_{\text{RMS}}|^2 = 2\pi f \epsilon_0 \epsilon' \tan(\delta) |E_{\text{RMS}}|^2$$

As the loose powder fuses into dense, non-porous basaltic ceramic glass ($>2.85\,\text{g/cm}^3$), its effective RF cavity impedance drops along a characteristic sigmoidal S-curve from $Z_{\text{initial}} \approx 1250\,\Omega$ to $Z_{\text{final}} \approx 85\,\Omega$. The fatal industry failure mode: internal degassing of entrapped volatile pockets or differential thermal gradients causes localized "Z-stalls"—impedance stops declining despite continued energy absorption. Existing open-loop systems fail to detect this, producing brittle blocks with internal voids and microcracks that fail catastrophically under rocket plume blast impingement.
