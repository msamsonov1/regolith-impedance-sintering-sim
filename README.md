RegoMelt: Lunar Regolith Microwave Sintering Complex Impedance AuditorExplore Live Interactive Digital Twin | NASA SBIR/STTR Topic: TX07.01 / TX12.01 | Proprietary NoticeA real-time closed-loop multi-physics digital twin and high-temperature complex impedance auditor for 2.45 GHz microwave sintering of in-situ lunar regolith into structural ceramics exceeding 30 MPa compressive strength.Executive OverviewArtemis surface permanence requires scalable in-situ manufacturing of launch and landing pads, blast deflection berms, and radiation-shielded habitat blocks without hauling heavy payload mass from Earth. However, raw lunar regolith under hard vacuum has an extremely low thermal conductivity:k ≈ 0.002 to 0.01 W / (m · K)Traditional radiative and conductive surface heating inevitably causes surface vitrification and volatile boil-off before the brick interior can consolidate.RegoMelt utilizes microwave volumetric heating driven by sub-micron nanophase metallic iron (np-Fe⁰) embedded inside lunar impact agglutinates. As the regolith heats and grain boundaries fuse into dense basaltic glass (> 2.85 g/cm³), its microwave power dissipation evolves dynamically:                  2

The Core Problem Solved: The "Z-Stall"During open-loop microwave sintering, internal volatile degassing pockets and differential thermal gradients form hollow cavities that halt dielectric densification—a condition termed a Z-Stall. Existing hardware fails to catch this in real time, yielding structural blocks riddled with hidden internal microcracks.RegoMelt audits the complex impedance trajectory Z(E) along its non-linear sigmoidal decline, dynamically modulating magnetron duty cycles and phase shifters to collapse internal voids before micro-fractures nucleate.Key Capabilities3D Electromagnetic Cavity Simulation (Three.js): Real-time spatial tracking of 2.45 GHz multi-mode standing wave nodes, localized volumetric heating cores, and workpiece consolidation.Live (E, Z) S-Curve Tracking (Chart.js): Dynamic plotting of real-time complex impedance decay from unconsolidated powder (Z ≈ 1250 Ω) down to dense basalt ceramic (Z ≈ 85 Ω).Closed-Loop Anomaly Mitigation:Void Degas Detection (Z-Stall): Dynamically throttles and pulses RF duty cycles to allow degassing channels to collapse without thermal shock.Thermal Runaway Suppression: Quenches high-temperature loss-tangent spikes before structural vaporization occurs.Turnkey NASA SBIR/STTR Phase I Proposal ($225,000): Fully formatted technical proposal, work breakdown structure, and milestone schedule aligned with subtopics TX07.01 (ISRU) and TX12.01 (In-Space Manufacturing).Zero Dependencies: Fully static, lightweight browser delivery powered by Tailwind CSS, Three.js, Chart.js, and KaTeX.Mathematical FormulationThe densification auditor models complex impedance decay as a function of cumulative absorbed energy E and nanophase iron content:                           (Z_initial - Z_final)
